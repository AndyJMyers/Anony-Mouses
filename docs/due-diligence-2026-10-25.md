## 1. Hosting: IO Zoom remains a candidate

The [Linux VPS page](https://www.iozoom.com/linux-vps/) lists a self-managed KVM plan with **4GB RAM, 40GB SSD, 4 CPUs and 4TB transfer for $18/month**. Its [AUP](https://www.iozoom.com/acceptable-use-policy/) permits lawful adult material on VPS/dedicated services, but not shared hosting. That is encouraging policy fit, not written acceptance of our exact UK service or confirmation of London availability.

The [cancellation instructions](https://www.iozoom.com/client/knowledgebase/40/How-do-I-cancel-my-account.html) use the client portal. [Terms](https://www.iozoom.com/terms-of-service/), sections 3, 5 and 6, provide recurring host billing, cancellation before renewal to avoid another charge, and generally no prorated refund. A limited seven-day VPS guarantee has exclusions; it is not a general trial/refund promise. The KB and terms differ about managed plans, so request confirmation for the exact self-managed order. Cancelling a PayPal mandate alone does not cancel the service.

**Open:** actual London stock, checkout/renewal taxes and extras, contracting entity/DPA, support access countries, log/deletion periods and help with incidents. Published terms describe US entities inconsistently; confirm the actual counterparty. A low price and support advertisement do not establish support quality or application management. No control-panel licence is needed for our stack.

The plan advertises snapshots; the [backup KB](https://www.iozoom.com/client/knowledgebase/21/Do-you-provide-backups.html) describes internal emergency full-server recovery, not customer file restores. Confirm retention/restore access and cost. Keep independently controlled encrypted backups and test recovery; separately approve any external private-media/backup supplier and its transfers/deletion terms. A 40GB disk is not permanent nationwide media capacity.

## 2. Age assurance: Didit is inexpensive to evaluate, privacy is unresolved

[Current pricing](https://didit.me/pricing/) lists age estimation at **$0.10/check**, including passive liveness. Its [announced 1 November 2026 change](https://didit.me/blog/new-pricing-november-2026/) retains that list price and introduces **$10 shared monthly credit**, replacing the old 500-check allowance. Pay-as-you-go remains available without a monthly subscription/minimum; credit eligibility and actual workflow charges need confirmation. Do not buy a paid tier simply to obtain age estimation.

**Illustration, not a quote:** 1,000 checks at $0.10 are $100 before tax, retries, fallback or other modules. If eligible for the announced $10 credit and using no other modules, net usage would be $90. Attempts can exceed paying customers. Failed sessions may contain billable checks. Reuse an appropriate valid age result rather than automatically rechecking every renewal.

The [verification privacy notice](https://didit.me/terms/verification-privacy-notice/) gives general verification data an unlimited default unless changed. It distinguishes operational session deletion from complete privacy erasure: optional retained biometric templates can survive session deletion and need finite expiry/purge. A deletion request is not completed until all in-scope stores confirm purge or approved beyond-use handling. **Do not enable a retained biometric gallery/template for our proposed age-only workflow.**

The [deletion help](https://help.didit.me/data-privacy/delete-verification-data) offers console/API session deletion, but identifies separate blocklists, queued webhooks and audit records; activity logs can remain for 365 days. Its immediate-deletion description is not proof of immediate erasure across every store. Obtain one binding explanation covering documents, selfies, estimates, failed/abandoned attempts, templates, logs, backups and independent-controller exceptions.

**Open:** acceptance of this business; UK-relevant effectiveness, challenge-age buffer, bypass/bias testing, accessible fallback and appeals; minimum returned fields; all billable steps; DPA/entities/subprocessors/countries and UK-transfer safeguards. Our app should retain only the account-bound over-18 result, necessary reference, time and policy/method version. Do not import identity documents, exact DOB, raw age estimates or selfies into patron profiles. Prove minimisation in an integration test; provider processing must still be disclosed honestly.

## 3. Payments: classification and cash required are the commercial gate

CCBill advertises [dating processing](https://ccbill.com/industries/dating). Its [pricing](https://ccbill.com/pricing) lists **$950 Visa and $1,000 Mastercard annual registrations for certain MCCs**, not every merchant. If both apply, that is **$1,950 upfront/year**, equivalent to $162.50/month for budgeting but not a monthly payment offer. Do not assume either universal applicability or exemption.

Its [Visa VIRP guidance](https://ccbill.com/doc/visa-integrity-risk-program-virp) includes dating MCC 7273 and describes a $950 initial application charge and additional Visa assessments of $0.10 plus 0.10% per transaction. Obtain the exact UK classification and establish whether application/annual fees overlap; do not add them twice or treat these assessments as the whole processing price.

**Open:** written acceptance of the full model, sole trader/company eligibility, GBP one-off £50/£20/£10 purchases, seven-day deferred activation, all fixed/percentage/FX/cross-border fees, minimums, reserves, payout timing, refunds and chargebacks, statement descriptor and necessary buyer details. Confirm age, image-consent and content-review conditions for non-explicit DMs. Rules requiring blanket private-image review could conflict with our privacy promise. A reserve restricts cash availability even where it is not a processing expense.

Stripe is not a ready fallback. Its [restricted list](https://stripe.com/legal/restricted-businesses) includes dating; its [FAQ](https://support.stripe.com/questions/prohibited-and-restricted-businesses-list-faqs) places sexually oriented dating in adult-services restrictions. **Our inference:** the sexual focus makes ordinary self-service eligibility unsafe to assume, despite our pornography ban. Only a truthful, specific written eligibility decision could justify proceeding; a standard tariff is not approval.

## 5. Legal, privacy and operational checks

- **Online safety and age access:** [Ofcom's dating guidance](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/dating-and-social-discovery-know-the-online-safety-risks-rules-and-how-to-comply) covers dating interactions, including private messaging. A ban alone does not remove duties. [Children's access guidance](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/childrens-access-assessment-duties-under-the-online-safety-act) allows the stage-one exclusion only with highly effective age assurance and effective access controls. Validate our agreed gate, complete the assessments and applicable risk-based measures; this is not a claim that every introduction service must demand a passport. [Ofcom's register](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/online-safety-regulatory-documents) lists code amendments issued 12 October with effect 21 days later: recheck commencement and applicability for our November work and December launch.
- **Sensitive data:** [ICO guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/a-guide-to-lawful-basis/special-category-data/) includes sex-life/orientation data. Document both an Article 6 basis and an Article 9 condition, including specific explicit consent where chosen, before collection. Complete the DPIA, controller/contact details, processor contracts, transfers, access controls and rights handling. A pseudonym is not anonymisation. Preserve the README's field-by-field privacy matrix; proposed limits are not yet live guarantees.
- **Reports and evidence:** finish restricted review, decisions, appeals and prompt escalation. The [NCA reporting duty](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/duty-to-report-child-sexual-exploitation-and-abuse-csea-content-know-the-rules-and-how-to-comply) is already in force for regulated user-to-user services. Keep statutory evidence separate from ordinary 14-day messages and 100 keepsakes. Regulation 8's exceptional retention requirements are described in the [README](../README.md#deletion-evidence-and-the-limits-of-discretion); the prototype's general 90-day report timer is insufficient. No blanket routine reading or extra chat archive is proposed.
- **Consumer checkout:** [GOV.UK distance-selling guidance](https://www.gov.uk/online-and-distance-selling-for-businesses/online-selling) and [government Consumer Contracts guidance](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a817b92ed915d74e33fe73a/bis-13-1368-consumer-contracts-information-cancellation-and-additional-payments-regulations-guidance.pdf) require clear seller/price/term information and applicable cancellation rights. For this proposed online service, design around the 14-day cancellation period from contracting; starting within it needs the required express request/information and lawful proportionate charging where applicable. A seven-day delayed start does not restart or remove that period. Manual renewal does not mean “no refunds”. Implement durable confirmation, cancellation and refund flows before charging.
- **Marketing:** follow the [ICO electronic-mail rules](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-on-direct-marketing-using-electronic-mail/how-do-we-comply-with-the-pecr-electronic-mail-marketing-rules/). Separate corporate from individual subscribers, lawful basis from PECR consent requirements, and campaign contacts from patron intimate data; include sender identity and a working opt-out/suppression route. Public addresses are not blanket permission. Seek student-union distribution/advertising permission, target adults, make no guaranteed-date/sex claims and do not imply endorsements. No outreach has been sent.

Report-led review is our intended privacy approach, not proof of compliance by itself: the service-specific assessments must identify any further proportionate preventive measures and verify that our controls work.
